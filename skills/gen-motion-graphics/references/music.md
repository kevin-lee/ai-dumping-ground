# Music

Find background music that is free for commercial use, fits under a voice, and has a beat the visuals can lock to. Record proof of its license.

## What to look for (default)

Fast-paced, rhythmic lo-fi hip hop: upbeat yet smooth, a steady drum beat from early in the track, 85 to 110 beats per minute (BPM), and instrumental, with no vocals that would fight the narration. "Calmer" means 75 to 90 BPM with softer drums. The user's own preferences (mood, genre, a track they own) override all of this.

## Three ways to get music

1. **A found track with a generated beat on top** (the default). A real CC0 production carries the melody, chords and sound, and a beat synthesized for this video adds drive. Most of this file is about finding the track. The beat comes from `mix_audio.py`, so there is nothing more to fetch. See "The generated beat" below.
2. **An original beat only**, composed in code with `scripts/compose_music.py`. Everything is synthesized there: electric piano chords, bass, kick, snare, swung hats, a soft plucked melody, vinyl crackle and tape wobble. Nothing to license, nothing for Content ID to match, and the track is made in video time for this narration. The drums drop on the chosen word, `--breaks` take them out where the narration turns, and it ends on a bar line with the mix.
   - `--bpm` 85 to 100 for the user's usual "fast-paced but smooth" (default 92). `--style calm` gives fewer notes and softer drums.
   - `--seed` picks the key and one of three chord progressions (I-vi-ii-V bright, IV-iii-vi-I warm, ii-V-I-vi jazzy). Fix `--key` or `--progression` when the user asks for a mood.
   - The melody plays in every other phrase under the narration and in every phrase after the last word.
   - It writes `music.json` in the shape `analyze_music.py` writes, so mixing, the beat pulse and drum activity work the same.
   - Record it in `sources/music/MUSIC.md`: an original composition generated by this skill, the command, seed, key and BPM. Say that it contains no third-party material, so the user may dedicate it to the public domain (CC0) or keep the rights.
   - You cannot hear it. Offer a found track as the alternative if the user finds it too synthetic.
3. **A found track only** (`--beat off` in `mix_audio.py`), when the user wants the track exactly as it is.

## The generated beat

`mix_audio.py --beat light` (the default for a found track) synthesizes drums with `compose_music.py`'s instruments and lays them on the edited track in video time:

- **Locked to the track.** `analyze_music.py` fits the beat grid to the track's attacks to about a millisecond and finds which beats have a kick or a snare, how late the snare sits and where the hi-hats fall (the swing). The mixer shifts the whole beat onto the track's attacks, then moves every kick and clap onto the hit it doubles. It prints how far it moved them. A loose track gets no beat: when the track's own beat strays more than 8 ms from a steady grid (`grid_jitter_ms` in `music.json`), the mixer leaves the beat out and says why.
- **Following the track.** Hi-hats play the track's eighths (and sixteenths when it has them) in its swing. Claps double its snares, and kicks add punch to its downbeats. Where the track's drums rest, the beat rests too. A snare fill and a crash mark the drums coming in on the drop and after every breakdown, so whole-bar cuts that move a breakdown onto a turn in the narration also move those accents.
- **Drums only.** The composed piano, bass and melody stay out. They would clash with the track's key and chords, which cannot be read reliably from a recording.
- **Levels.** Each part is set against the track's own energy in its band, so the beat supports the track instead of covering it. `--beat light`, the default the user confirmed after listening, puts the kick 10 dB, the claps 9 dB and the hi-hats 12 dB under the track. `--beat strong` (6, 6 and 9 dB under) adds a kick on beat 3 and a snare under the claps. A composed track never gets one, because it has drums of its own.
- **License.** The beat is original and contains no third-party material, so the track's license is the only one that matters. Mention the beat in the "How it is used" section of `MUSIC.md`.
- You cannot hear the result. Report the beat setting and the lock line from the mixer, and offer `--beat strong` or `--beat off` as the alternatives.

## Licenses

| License | Use it? |
|---|---|
| CC0 1.0 / public domain | Yes. Commercial use is fine and no credit is required. This is the default |
| CC BY | Only if no CC0 track fits. Credit is required (title, artist, license and link in the video description), so tell the user |
| CC BY-NC, any ND | No. NC rules out product and monetised videos, and ND forbids putting music under a video |
| Pixabay Content License | Avoid. It is not CC0, many tracks are registered with Content ID (claims even on legal use), and it blocks downloads by script |
| YouTube Audio Library | No. The files may not be redistributed outside YouTube, and this video also ships as an HTML page |
| Internet Archive "public domain" uploads | Only as a lead. The uploader typed the license, so it is not proof |
| FreePD.com | Closed in 2025. Its leftovers on the Internet Archive have weak provenance |

Check the license on the **track** page, not the artist or album. One artist can mix licenses (John Bartmann has CC0 albums and a CC BY one).

## Choosing

You cannot listen, so choose on data and let the user judge:

- Match the tempo to the narration's energy. 95 to 110 BPM suits a lively intro, 85 to 95 a relaxed explainer.
- Prefer tracks whose drums start within the first few bars (`analyze_music.py` shows the drop) and that last long enough. Shorter tracks loop whole bars cleanly, but a long track means fewer repeats.
- For the generated beat, the track must be steady: `analyze_music.py` prints how far the track's attacks stray from the grid. Windows Down (5 ms), Doodles (2 ms) and happy lofi day (4 ms) all passed. Over 8 ms, choose another track or go without the beat.
- Check `.ai/video/*/sources/music/MUSIC.md` for tracks used in the user's other videos. Mention it, and reuse one only if the user wants a consistent channel sound.
- Name the pick and two alternatives in the final report, each with its page link so the user can preview them. Swapping later costs only a re-mix and a rebuild.

## Download and keep proof

For FMA and OpenGameArt track pages:

```sh
uv run --script <skill>/scripts/fetch_music.py "<track page URL>" sources/music
```

It finds the file through the page's own track data, checks the license (refusing NC and ND), and downloads the exact file. It saves a copy of the page and asks the Wayback Machine for a snapshot, then writes `sources/music/MUSIC.md` with the title, artist, album, both URLs, the license, the SHA-256 of the file, the date, the snapshot and a credit line. Add a short "How it is used" section to `MUSIC.md` after mixing (tempo, the drop, any cuts, the ending).

For other sources, download by hand with `curl -L -A "Mozilla/5.0" -o sources/music/<name>.mp3 <file URL>` and write `MUSIC.md` with the same fields. FMA answers 403 to clients without a browser-like user agent.

CC0 and Creative Commons recordings cannot be registered with YouTube's Content ID, but wrong claims still happen. Tell the user to keep `MUSIC.md`, the page copy and the snapshot, and to dispute any claim with them.
